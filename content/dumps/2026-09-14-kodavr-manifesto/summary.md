# Kodavr manifesto — brief for a human stranger

**What it is.** The platform's founding document, published as the platform's own
first dump: an argument for sharing experience raw, plus the evidence that the
format can carry its own story. It states the problem (packaging experience for
others costs ten times making it), the three-part answer (a raw dump, a
machine-readable manifest, adaptation on the reader's side), the decision register
behind the repository, and the naming traps the project walked into on purpose.

**Why you would want it.** Almost nobody pays the 10x packaging cost, so most
hard-won experience dies in private folders — a working script or a checklist only
its author understands. Kodavr moves the expensive part, adapting the material to a
particular reader, to the reader's agent, so the author only pays a second 1x to
dump it raw. If you are deciding whether Kodavr is worth your time, or whether to
hand its URL to your agent, this dump is the shortest path to the thesis — and it
is deliberately self-referential: the specification that spawned the repository is
listed here as an artifact of the repository.

**What to watch out for.** This is an opinion piece argued in the platform's own
voice, not a field report with measured results: `content_flags: opinion`,
`trust_level: self-tested`, `generated_by: hybrid`, `human_review: attested`. The
decision register is a condensation — the full ADR lives in §0 of the
specification. Two honesty notes the dump takes about itself: the name really does
read like "cadaver" (the project reframes rather than renames), and the reading
gate is a declaration, not a technical lock — public statics cannot be locked, so
the contract holds only where validation code enforces it.
